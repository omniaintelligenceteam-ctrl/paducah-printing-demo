The file check

Give your artwork room to print well.

  1. Confirm the finished size. Build your file at the intended size. Check page count, folds, and binding before settling the layout.
  2. Plan for trim and bleed. Artwork that reaches an edge usually needs to extend beyond the trim line. Confirm the required bleed and safe text area with us for your job.
  3. Check image resolution. For typical close-view print, aim for raster images around 300 dpi at their final printed size. Enlarging a small image does not add detail. Large format work may need different specifications.
  4. Discuss color. CMYK is a common starting point for full-color print. Ask us about the right color settings, profiles, and any spot colors before exporting. A screen preview and a printed sheet can look different.
  5. Keep type and graphics intact. Embed fonts in your PDF when permitted, and check that linked images are included. Keep a copy of your editable source files.
  6. Review the final PDF. Check spelling, dates, phone numbers, page order, image quality, and text near the edges. Ask which PDF export settings and proofing approach suit your project.

These are starting guidelines. The size, material, production method, and finishing can change a job’s file requirements. Call 270-443-5383 to confirm before sending final artwork.

A few useful answers

Before you upload.

What if my artwork is not ready?

Start with a call. Design services through Horizon Media Group can help with a new layout or preparing existing artwork. Tell us what you have and what you want to make.

Should I add crop marks or a specific amount of bleed?

Confirm those settings with us for your project. The appropriate export settings depend on the finished size, printing method, and any folding, binding, or specialty finishing.

Can I use an image from a website?

Use the original, highest-resolution file you have permission to reproduce. A small web image or screenshot may look soft in print. Check its resolution at the size it will actually appear.

How should I prepare foil, embossing, or die-cut artwork?

Discuss those details before finalizing the file. Ask how to supply the artwork for each finish and where it should sit in relation to folds, trim, and printed content.
